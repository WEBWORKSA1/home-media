Most people buy a TV that's too small. In the shop, surrounded by 98-inch displays, a 55-inch looks generous. At home, nine feet away on the sofa, it looks like a window across the room. Here's how to choose with numbers instead of guesswork.

## The two guidelines that matter

Screen size is really about **how much of your field of view the picture fills**.

- **SMPTE** (the Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers) recommends the screen fill at least **30°** of your horizontal view. Below that, it feels small.
- **THX** recommends about **40°** from the best seat for a cinematic, immersive feel.

Between those two is the sweet spot. We suggest **35°** for mixed TV and movie watching, 40° for movie fans and gamers, and 30° for a room that's mostly news and sport.

## Ready reckoner

Distances are from your eyes to the screen, for 16:9 TVs.

| Screen | Immersive (40°) | All-round (35°) | Max (30°) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 55" | 5.5 ft | 6.3 ft | 7.5 ft |
| 65" | 6.5 ft | 7.5 ft | 8.8 ft |
| 75" | 7.5 ft | 8.6 ft | 10.2 ft |
| 85" | 8.5 ft | 9.8 ft | 11.5 ft |
| 98" | 9.8 ft | 11.3 ft | 13.3 ft |

For your exact room, use the [TV size and viewing distance calculator](screen-size.html). It works in feet or metres and also handles projector screens.

## Does resolution change the answer?

Resolution decides how close you can sit **before you see pixels**, and how close you must sit **to see all the detail**. A person with 20/20 vision resolves about one arc-minute (1/60 of a degree). Do the maths and you find:

- On a 65-inch TV, full 4K detail is visible within about 4.2 ft; full 1080p detail within about 8.5 ft.
- At a typical 9 ft, a 65-inch 4K TV doesn't look sharper than 1080p because of resolution alone.

So why buy 4K? Because modern 4K TVs bring **HDR, wider colour, better processing and more brightness**, and that's what you actually see at normal distances. The lesson is not "4K doesn't matter" but "**size and picture quality matter more than pixel count**".

## Other factors

### Mounting height

Put the centre of the screen near seated eye level, about **40–42 inches** off the floor. Mounting high (above a fireplace, for example) strains your neck and pushes you outside the TV's best viewing angle. If the fireplace is the only wall, use a pull-down mount.

### Room light

A bright room favours a brighter TV with a good anti-glare coating; a dark room lets you go bigger with a projector. Compare them in [projector vs TV](guides/projector-vs-tv.html).

### Seating width

If people sit far to the side, choose a panel with wide viewing angles (OLED and some LCDs with wide-angle filters hold colour and contrast off-axis far better).

### Furniture and walls

Check the TV's width, not just its diagonal. A 75-inch TV is about 66 inches wide; an 85-inch about 74 inches.

## Our recommendation

Measure your seat distance, multiply by roughly **8.7** (in feet) to get a starting diagonal in inches for an all-round 35°, then check the result in the calculator. Nine feet × 8.7 ≈ 78 inches. If budget forces a choice between a bigger screen and a slightly better model, most people are happier with the bigger screen, as long as it still has good HDR.
