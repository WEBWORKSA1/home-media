HDMI is digital, and most of the time it simply works. It stops working when a signal needs more bandwidth than a port or cable can carry, or when audio needs a feature the TV doesn't have. Here's what matters.

## HDMI versions in plain English

| Version | Usable data (approx.) | What it's for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HDMI 2.0 | ~14.4 Gbps (18 Gbps link) | 4K at 60 Hz with HDR (usually 4:2:0 or 4:2:2) |
| HDMI 2.1 | up to ~42.7 Gbps (48 Gbps link) | 4K at 120 Hz, 8K, VRR, ALLM, full 4:4:4 colour at high frame rates |

Many TVs implement HDMI 2.1 ports at **40 Gbps**, which carries 4K 120 Hz at 10-bit, the format modern consoles use. Check any signal in the [HDMI bandwidth checker](hdmi.html).

## Gaming features

- **4K at 120 Hz:** smoother motion; needs HDMI 2.1 ports on console and TV, and a capable cable.
- **VRR (variable refresh rate):** syncs the TV to the console's frame rate to avoid tearing.
- **ALLM (auto low latency mode):** switches the TV to game mode automatically.

If you route a console through an AV receiver, the receiver needs HDMI 2.1 too, or connect the console directly to the TV and send audio back via eARC.

## ARC vs eARC

**ARC** (audio return channel) sends sound from the TV back to a soundbar or receiver over the same HDMI cable. It carries compressed formats, including Dolby Digital Plus with Atmos.

**eARC** (enhanced ARC) adds much more bandwidth, enough for **lossless** Dolby TrueHD with Atmos and DTS-HD Master Audio, from TV apps or devices plugged into the TV. For the best sound when sources connect to the TV, both TV and soundbar/receiver need eARC.

## Cables: certified, not expensive

HDMI cables either carry the signal cleanly or fail with dropouts, sparkles or a black screen. There's no "warmer picture" from a pricier cable. Buy by certification:

| Need | Buy |
| --- | --- |
| 1080p, 4K at 30 Hz | High Speed |
| 4K at 60 Hz HDR (18 Gbps) | Premium High Speed |
| 4K at 120 Hz, 8K (48 Gbps) | Ultra High Speed |

For long in-wall runs (a ceiling projector, a rack in another room), use **certified active optical** cables, which handle distance far better than passive copper at high bandwidth. Run them through conduit so they can be replaced later.

## Settings people miss

- **Enhanced HDMI / input signal plus / UHD colour:** enable it on the TV input your 4K or HDR source uses, or the source may fall back to lower formats.
- **TV audio output:** set to pass-through/bitstream or auto, and enable eARC in the TV's sound settings.
- **HDMI-CEC:** enable it (it has brand names like Anynet+, Bravia Sync or SimpLink) so one remote controls the system. Turn it off on a device that misbehaves.

## Troubleshooting checklist

1. Use the TV's HDMI 2.1 or eARC-labelled ports.
2. Swap in a certified cable rated for the signal.
3. Enable enhanced mode on that input.
4. Power-cycle devices in order: display, then receiver/soundbar, then source.
5. Update firmware on TV, receiver and source.
