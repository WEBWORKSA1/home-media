A good installer makes a system disappear into the room and sound right from day one. A poor one leaves visible cables, uneven sound and a stack of remotes. Here's how to tell them apart before you sign.

## Know your scope first

Installers quote more accurately, and more competitively, when you arrive with a clear brief: room size, seat position, the screen size you want, the speaker layout, budget and timeline. ## Where to find candidates

- **Trade directories.** CEDIA lists member integrators by location; brands such as Control4 run dealer locators for their systems.
- **Specialist retailers** with design and install teams.
- **Home-services marketplaces** for smaller jobs like TV mounting.
- **Referrals** from neighbours and local forums.
- **Home.Media's network:** request introductions to up to three vetted local pros through the plan.

## Vetting checklist

- Licensed and insured for the work involved (especially electrical)
- Relevant certifications (CEDIA, ISF or THX for calibration, manufacturer certifications)
- Photos or visits of **similar** rooms, not just showroom shots
- References you can call
- Clear about who does the work (staff or subcontractors)
- Written warranty on labour and a defined support period

## Questions to ask

1. What exactly is included in the quote: equipment, wiring, mounts, labour, calibration, programming, clean-up?
2. Will you calibrate the picture and run speaker/room correction? Do you measure?
3. Where will each speaker, the subwoofer(s) and the equipment rack go, and why?
4. How will cables be hidden? What's left accessible for future upgrades?
5. Who do I call if something stops working, and how fast do you respond?
6. How long will it take, and what will the room look like during the work?
7. Can I supply any equipment? How does that affect warranty?

## What a good quote looks like

- **Itemised** equipment with **model numbers**
- Labour broken out by task
- Cable types and lengths listed (e.g. certified Ultra High Speed HDMI for 4K120)
- Calibration and programming as separate lines
- Payment schedule tied to milestones
- Timeline and warranty terms

## Red flags

- Vague "home theater package" with no model numbers
- Pressure to sign today for a "discount"
- No insurance or reluctance to show licensing
- Dismissing room correction or calibration as unnecessary
- Insisting on expensive cables as a performance upgrade

## Compare fairly

Line up the quotes in a simple table: core components, labour, wiring, calibration, support, timeline and total. The cheapest quote often leaves out calibration or cable management; the most expensive sometimes pads equipment. Ask each installer about the differences and see who explains clearly.
