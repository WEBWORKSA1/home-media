"How much does a home theater cost?" has three honest answers, because there are three very different projects. Here they are with real numbers.

## The three tiers

| Project | What's included | Typical total |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living-room upgrade | 65–85" TV, soundbar or 5.1, mounting, streaming | ~$1,000–$6,000 |
| Media room | 77–98" TV or projector, Atmos 5.1.2–7.1.4, light control, some acoustics | ~$6,000–$20,000 |
| Dedicated home theater | Projector + large screen, 7.2.4+ Atmos, construction, acoustics, seating, lighting, calibration | ~$20,000–$60,000+ |

Published 2026 cost guides put a professionally installed home theater at an average of about **$20,000**, typically **$10,000–$60,000**, with dedicated room build-outs at **$80–$200 per square foot** (Angi). Model your own combination in the [cost estimator](cost-estimator.html).

## Where the money goes

### Display

TVs range from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands at the largest sizes. Projectors span from about $1,000 entry models to high-end units over $20,000, and the screen adds anywhere from a couple of hundred to many thousands of dollars.

### Sound

- Soundbar with subwoofer: ~$250–$1,500
- 5.1 receiver system: ~$800–$3,500
- Atmos 5.1.2–7.1.4: ~$2,000–$8,000
- Reference systems: $8,000 and up

### Construction and room

In dedicated rooms, construction often costs more than the electronics: framing, drywall, electrical, HVAC, risers, doors and soundproofing. That's where the $80–$200 per square foot figure comes from.

### Seating, acoustics, lighting

- Theater seating: from about $500 to $20,000 for complex multi-row installs
- Acoustic treatment, installed: about $300–$1,000 for basic panels; full designs cost more
- Lighting: about $150–$2,500

## Where to save, and where not to

**Save on:** speaker cables (buy correctly sized copper, not "audiophile" brands), HDMI cables (buy certified, not expensive), seating (a good sofa beats cheap recliners), extra channels you can add later.

**Don't save on:** screen size relative to your seat, the front three speakers, a receiver with good room correction, in-wall wiring while walls are open, and a second subwoofer in larger rooms.

## Phase it

1. **Phase 1:** display, front speakers (or soundbar), subwoofer, wiring for the final layout.
2. **Phase 2:** surrounds and overheads.
3. **Phase 3:** second subwoofer, acoustic panels, calibration.
