How to read the chart

Each line is a frequency where one pair of walls (or floor and ceiling) resonates. Taller lines are lower-order, stronger modes. Lines bunched together mean those notes will be exaggerated; wide gaps below about 150 Hz mean some bass notes will sound thin. An evenly spread pattern is what you want.

The formula is f = n × c ÷ (2 × L), where c is the speed of sound (about 1,125 ft/s or 343 m/s), L is the dimension and n is 1, 2, 3 and so on.

Practical fixes, cheapest first

  1. Move the seat. Avoid the exact middle of the room and keep your head off the back wall. Around 38% of the room length from the front or back wall is a classic starting point.
  2. Move or add subwoofers. Two subs at the front-wall quarter points, or front and back, smooth bass across seats far better than one.
  3. Run room correction. Modern receivers' EQ can tame peaks (it can't fill deep nulls; that's what sub placement is for).
  4. Add bass traps. Thick, corner-mounted traps reduce how long bass rings.
