Placement principles that matter most

  1. Front three first. Left, centre and right carry most dialogue and music. Keep them at roughly ear height, the same distance from your seat where possible, and toed-in toward you.
  2. Surrounds slightly above ear height. One to two feet above seated ears avoids sound being blocked by people and furniture.
  3. Overheads in line with the fronts. Ceiling speakers usually sit in line with, or slightly inside, the front left/right pair. Top-front speakers go in front of you at about 45° up; top-rear behind you at a similar angle.
  4. Upfiring modules are a valid fallback. If you rent or can't cut the ceiling, Dolby Atmos-enabled (upfiring) speakers bounce sound off a flat ceiling. They work best on ceilings between about 7.5 and 12 ft.
  5. Let room correction finish the job. Enter distances, then run Audyssey, Dirac, YPAO or ARC. It sets levels and timing far better than guesswork.
