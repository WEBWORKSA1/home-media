Streaming & cord-cutting

How to Cut the Cord in 2026 (and Actually Save Money)

A practical plan to replace cable or satellite: what you watch, antenna for locals, live TV vs on-demand services, streaming devices, internet needs and the rotation strategy that saves the most.

By the Home.Media Editorial Team · Updated Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer

  • List what you actually watch before choosing services.
  • Get local channels free with an antenna where reception allows.
  • Only pay for live TV streaming if you need cable news or sports channels.
  • Rotate on-demand services instead of keeping them all.

Cord-cutting is no longer automatically cheap. Streaming prices have climbed, and stacking every service can cost as much as cable. Done deliberately, though, most households still save hundreds a year. Here's the plan.

Step 1: Write down what you actually watch

For two weeks, note the shows, channels and sports you watch. Most households find that a handful of things matter and the rest is background. Split the list into:

  • Local broadcast (network news, network shows, many big sports events)
  • Cable channels (cable news, some sports networks, lifestyle channels)
  • On-demand shows and films (series and films on the streaming apps)

Step 2: Get local channels free with an antenna

In many areas, a simple antenna brings in the major broadcast networks in HD for free. Check reception at your address first. Our antenna guide explains how and which antenna type to buy.

Step 3: Decide if you need live TV streaming

A live TV streaming service replaces the cable box: dozens of channels, cloud DVR, sports networks. It's also the most expensive piece, typically $45–$90 a month for the main services. You need one only if your list includes cable news or cable-only sports. If not, skip it; that's the biggest saving available.

Step 4: Pick on-demand services, then rotate

On-demand services (Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, Prime Video, Apple TV, Peacock, Paramount+ and others) are month-to-month. You don't need them all at once. The rotation strategy:

  1. Keep one or two services active.
  2. Watch what you want on them.
  3. Cancel, and switch to the next one with something you want to see.

Ad-supported tiers save more where you don't mind ads. Plug your choices into the streaming cost calculator to see monthly and yearly totals against your current bill.

Step 5: Add free services

Free ad-supported apps (such as Tubi, Pluto TV and The Roku Channel) carry thousands of films and live-style channels for $0. If you have your own film collection, a home media server puts it on every TV.

Step 6: Get the hardware right

  • A streaming device for each TV if the built-in apps are slow or missing services. One device platform across the house keeps things simple.
  • A network that can keep up. One 4K stream needs about 15 Mbps; a busy household needs much more. Size it with the bandwidth planner.
  • An over-the-air DVR (optional) if you want to record antenna channels.

Step 7: Cancel cable the smart way

  • Return equipment and get receipts.
  • Ask for the internet-only price; negotiate if it jumps.
  • Check for early-termination fees and your billing cycle so you don't pay for an extra month.

A sample "save money" setup

Piece Monthly
Antenna for local channels $0 (one-time purchase)
One ad-free on-demand service, rotated ~$11–$20
One ad-supported service ~$8–$12
Free ad-supported apps $0
Total ~$19–$32

Frequently asked questions

Is cord-cutting cheaper than cable?

It can be, by a lot, or barely at all. Replacing cable with a live TV service plus several ad-free apps can approach a cable bill. Pairing an antenna with one or two rotating services and free apps is where the big savings are.

Can I keep my cable company's internet?

Usually yes. Ask for the internet-only price before cancelling TV; bundles can hide how much each part costs.

What about live sports?

Sports are the hardest part. Rights are spread across broadcast networks (antenna), live TV services, and individual apps. List your must-watch teams and leagues and check who carries them before cancelling.
