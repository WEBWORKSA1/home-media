Small Room & Apartment Home Theater Ideas

How to build a great home theater in a bedroom, small living room or rental: screen sizing, compact and wireless sound, neighbour-friendly bass, renter-safe mounting and light control.

By the Home.Media Editorial Team · Updated Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer

  • Small rooms need smaller screens than you'd think, but 55–75 inches still fits most.
  • A compact Atmos soundbar with wireless rears is the easiest renter-friendly surround.
  • Control bass with placement and night mode; consider headphones for late nights.
  • Use renter-safe mounting: TV stands, floor-standing frames and adhesive cable channels.

A small room isn't a compromise; it can be an advantage. You need less power, less screen and less treatment to fill it. The trick is choosing compact gear and placing it well.

Size the screen correctly

In small rooms people sit closer, so a big screen fills your view quickly. At 6–7 ft, a 55–65 inch TV already reaches the recommended 30–40° viewing angle. Check your exact distance in the screen size calculator.

If you want a bigger picture without a bigger TV, a short-throw or ultra-short-throw projector sits close to the wall. Compare options in projector vs TV.

Sound options that fit

Option Space needed Wiring Neighbour-friendly
Compact Atmos soundbar Just the TV cabinet One HDMI Good, with night mode
Soundbar + wireless rears + sub Small rear spots Power only for rears Moderate
Small 5.1 satellites + receiver Wall or stand space Speaker wires Moderate
Wireless headphones None None Excellent

The soundbar-with-wireless-rears route gives real surround without wires across the floor, which is ideal for renters. See soundbar vs surround sound.

Keep bass under control

Bass travels through walls and floors. In apartments:

  • Put the subwoofer away from shared walls, on an isolation pad or feet.
  • Use night mode or dynamic range compression so explosions don't spike.
  • Turn the sub level down a few dB and let room correction balance it.
  • Use wireless headphones for late-night films.

Renter-safe mounting and cables

  • TV stand or floor-standing TV mount instead of drilling.
  • Adhesive cable raceways painted to match the wall.
  • Flat speaker wire under rugs, or wireless rears.
  • Check the lease; many allow small screw holes if patched on move-out.

Light control without renovations

Blackout curtains or cellular blinds make a huge difference for both TVs (less glare) and projectors (better contrast). A bias light behind the TV reduces eye strain at night.

Small-room acoustics

Small rooms often sound boomy and bright. A rug, curtains and a couple of fabric panels at the side-wall reflection points tame it. Our room acoustics guide shows how to find those points with a mirror.

Sample setups

  • Bedroom, under $1,500: 55–65" TV, compact Atmos soundbar, wireless headphones.
  • Apartment living room, $2,000–$4,000: 65–75" TV, soundbar with wireless rears and sub, blackout curtains.
  • Small den, $3,000–$6,000: 100" UST projector on an ALR screen, 3.1 or 5.1 compact speakers.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a projector in a small room?

Yes. A short-throw or ultra-short-throw projector creates a big image from close to the wall. Light control is the main requirement.

How do I avoid annoying neighbours?

Keep the subwoofer off shared walls and floors (use isolation feet), use night mode or dynamic range compression, and switch to wireless headphones after hours.

Is 5.1 possible in a small room?

Yes. Small satellite speakers or a soundbar with wireless rears work well. Keep surrounds slightly above ear height and set distances in the system menu.
