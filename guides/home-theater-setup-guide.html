How to Set Up a Home Theater: The Complete Beginner's Guide

A step-by-step plan for your first home theater: choosing the room, screen size, sound system, sources, wiring and calibration, in the order that avoids expensive mistakes.

By the Home.Media Editorial Team · Updated Nov 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

  • Start with the room and seat, not the gear: the seat decides screen size and speaker positions.
  • Spend first on the display and the front three speakers (or a good soundbar).
  • Plan cables and power before mounting anything; hide them once.
  • Finish with calibration: picture mode, speaker distances and room correction.

A home theater doesn't need a dedicated room or a five-figure budget. It needs a sensible order of decisions. Most disappointing setups come from buying gear first and discovering later that the screen is too small for the sofa, the speakers can't go where they should, or the cables can't be hidden. Here's the order that works.

1. Pick the room and the main seat

Everything follows from where you sit. Measure the distance from your eyes to the screen wall, the room's length, width and ceiling height, and note windows (glare) and doorways (speaker positions).

A few things make a room easier:

  • A solid screen wall with no window behind or opposite the screen.
  • The seat away from the back wall. Sitting right against it exaggerates bass and leaves no room for surrounds.
  • Some light control. Curtains or blinds help any display, and are essential for projectors.

2. Size the screen to the seat

Use the screen size calculator. As a rule, the screen should fill 30–40° of your field of view. At a typical 9 ft living-room distance that's roughly a 75–85 inch TV, bigger than many people expect. If you want more than about 85 inches, compare a projector in projector vs TV.

Mount the TV so the centre of the screen sits near seated eye level, around 40–42 inches from the floor for most sofas. Above a fireplace is usually too high.

3. Choose the sound system

There are three sensible tiers:

Option Best for Typical gear budget
Soundbar + subwoofer (Atmos) Living rooms, apartments, minimal wiring $250–$1,500
5.1 receiver system Real surround, upgradeable $800–$3,500
Dolby Atmos 5.1.2 / 7.1.4 Media rooms and dedicated theaters $2,000–$8,000+

Read soundbar vs surround sound if you're torn. If you go with separate speakers, plan positions with the speaker layout planner: front left and right about 22–30° either side of the centre speaker, surrounds beside and slightly behind you.

4. Choose your sources

Most people need just two or three:

  • A streaming device or the TV's apps. A dedicated streamer often updates faster and supports more formats than built-in apps.
  • A 4K Blu-ray player if you care about the best picture and lossless sound.
  • A games console, which needs HDMI 2.1 for 4K at 120 Hz. Check with the HDMI checker.
  • A media server like Plex or Jellyfin for your own library. See Plex vs Jellyfin vs Emby vs Kodi.

5. Plan connections and power

Decide where the "brain" lives. Either sources plug into the TV and audio goes to the soundbar or receiver over eARC, or sources plug into the receiver and one HDMI cable goes to the TV. eARC is the simpler route for most soundbar setups; a receiver hub is common in dedicated rooms.

Before you mount anything:

  • Run in-wall power and HDMI behind a wall-mounted TV using a code-compliant recessed power kit (never run a standard extension cord inside a wall).
  • Leave a spare conduit for future cables. It costs little now and saves a wall later.
  • Use Ethernet for the main TV or streamer if you can.

6. Install, then calibrate

Calibration is where good systems become great ones, and the basics are free:

  1. Picture: choose Filmmaker Mode, Movie or Cinema mode, turn off motion smoothing for films, and set brightness for your room. Our calibration basics guide walks through it.
  2. Sound: enter speaker distances, then run the receiver's room correction (Audyssey, Dirac, YPAO or ARC) with the microphone at ear height in the main seat.
  3. Bass: try the subwoofer crawl and consider a second subwoofer; it smooths bass across seats more than any setting can.

7. Budget realistically

A good living-room setup can come in under $2,000. Published 2026 cost guides put a professionally installed home theater at about $20,000 on average, typically $10,000–$60,000, with full room build-outs at $80–$200 per square foot. Use the cost estimator to see where your choices land, and read how much a home theater costs for the breakdown.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Screen too small for the seating distance.
  • Centre speaker forgotten. Dialogue clarity lives there.
  • Subwoofer stuffed in a corner and never moved.
  • Cables visible because nobody planned the wall.
  • Skipping room correction after buying good speakers.

Frequently asked questions

What's the minimum for a real home theater?

A screen big enough to fill about 30° of your view from your seat, and sound coming from in front and around you. A good Atmos soundbar with a subwoofer is a valid start; a 5.1 receiver system is a big step up.

Should I buy everything at once?

No. Buy the display and front sound first, then add surrounds, a second subwoofer or acoustic panels as budget allows. Plan the wiring for the final system from day one.

Do I need a receiver?

Only for separate speakers. A receiver (AVR) powers the speakers, decodes surround formats and switches sources. Soundbars build that in.
