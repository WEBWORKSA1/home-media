Screens & projectors

Projector vs TV: Which Is Right for Your Room?

An honest comparison of projectors and big TVs: picture size, brightness, contrast, HDR, room light, sound, cost per inch and maintenance, with a quick decision guide.

By the Home.Media Editorial Team · Updated Nov 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Quick answer

  • Up to about 85–98 inches, a TV wins on brightness, contrast and convenience.
  • Beyond 100 inches, a projector gives far more picture per dollar.
  • Projectors need light control; ultra-short-throw models plus an ALR screen help in living rooms.
  • Budget for the screen: it's half the picture.

"Projector or TV?" is really a question about size, light and how you use the room. Both can look superb. They just win in different rooms.

The short version

Big TV Projector + screen
Best size range Up to ~98" 100"–150"+
Bright room Excellent Hard (ALR screen helps)
Dark-room contrast Excellent (OLED best) Good to excellent (model dependent)
HDR punch Strong Limited by brightness
Setup Mount and go Mounting, alignment, screen
Picture per dollar at 100"+ Expensive Excellent

Where TVs win

  • Brightness. Modern Mini LED and OLED TVs reach HDR highlights that projectors can't match, so specular highlights (sunlight on water, neon signs) pop.
  • Daytime viewing. Ambient light barely affects a bright TV. It washes out a projector.
  • Simplicity. No throw distance, no screen, no fan noise, no alignment.
  • Contrast in mixed scenes. OLED's per-pixel light control is hard to beat.

Where projectors win

  • Scale. A 120-inch image at 12 ft fills about 40° of your view, true cinema scale. A TV that size costs many times more.
  • Comfort. Reflected light from a screen is easy on the eyes in a dark room.
  • Disappearing act. A retractable screen and ceiling-mounted projector leave the wall free when you're not watching.

Living rooms: the ultra-short-throw option

Ultra-short-throw (UST) projectors sit on a low cabinet inches from the wall. Paired with a ceiling-light-rejecting ALR screen, they can deliver a 100–120 inch picture in a living room with lamps on. They're the closest thing to a "giant TV" a projector gets. Two catches: the screen must be perfectly flat, and placement tolerance is tiny.

Check brightness before you buy

The number that decides whether a projector looks good is foot-lamberts (fL) at your seat, not lumens on the box:

fL ≈ lumens × screen gain ÷ screen area (sq ft)

Calibrated output is typically 25–40% below the rated lumens, so plan with a margin. In a dark room, aim for 16–30 fL (HDR wants the top of that range). With some room light, 30–50 fL or more. Run your numbers in the projector throw and brightness calculator.

Don't forget the screen and the room

The screen is half the picture. A good fixed-frame screen gives a flat, uniform image; ALR screens trade some viewing angle for contrast in lit rooms. Dark or mid-grey walls and ceiling near the screen stop light bouncing back and lifting black levels.

Sound and noise

Neither option sounds great on its own. TV speakers are small; projector speakers are worse and usually behind you. Budget for a soundbar or speakers either way. Some projectors have audible fans, so mount them away from the seat or choose a quiet laser model.

Decision guide

  • Mostly daytime and sport, family room: a 77–98 inch TV.
  • Movies at night, room you can darken: a 4K projector and a 110–130 inch screen.
  • Living room, want 100"+, lights on sometimes: UST projector with an ALR screen, or a 98–100 inch TV if budget allows.
  • Dedicated theater: projector, nearly always.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a projector in a bright room?

Partly. An ultra-short-throw projector with a ceiling-light-rejecting (ALR) screen copes with lamps on, but daylight still washes out contrast. For daytime viewing, a large TV is better.

Do projectors work for gaming?

Many modern 4K projectors have low-latency game modes, some with 120 Hz at lower resolutions. Check input lag figures before buying if you play competitively.

How long do projectors last?

Laser and LED models are typically rated for around 20,000 hours or more to half brightness. Lamp projectors need lamp replacements every few thousand hours.
